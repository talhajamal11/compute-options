# compute-options

Compute is the newest commodity to get a financial market. GPU-hours are bought and sold every
day, spot indices now exist, exchanges are preparing futures and options, and lenders already
finance GPU fleets against the cash flows they produce. I find this fascinating: it is a
commodity you can't store, whose supply is manufactured on a roughly annual product cycle,
whose price level is set by cost of supply on one side and scarcity on the other, and whose
counterparties are mostly young, highly levered companies. It is also a market where the usual
derivatives machinery has to be rebuilt from the economics, since there is no traded forward to
calibrate to.

This repo is my attempt to build that machinery end to end: collect every public compute price
series, construct a forward curve from first principles with explicit assumptions, price
forwards, options, structured notes and credit-linked deals on it, and measure how much to trust
the results.

The question it works on: *what is a forward on compute worth when there is only a noisy spot
index, no traded forward, and a counterparty who might not deliver, and how sure are we?*

## What the results say

- **Spot is far above the cost of supply** (about 2x for H100, 2.5x for B200 on central
  assumptions), so the curve is backwardated: scarcity rent decays towards a cost anchor that
  itself falls as new generations ship. How fast is the largest uncertainty, and the data barely
  constrain it.
- **The structural curve did not beat "no change" in the walk-forward test**, because the index
  rose over the sample. The revision log records how the priors were then adjusted.
- **Credit matters most where you pay first.** Prepaying a neocloud costs about 3% of the price;
  being owed under a take-or-pay costs well under 0.5%. A GPU-backed loan is nearly
  free of credit cost below about 60% loan-to-value and reprices fast above it.
- **Model uncertainty is the largest charge in a quote** for an investment-grade counterparty and
  about level with credit for a high-yield one. Market risk capital is small by comparison.
- **The biggest drivers are judgment inputs**, not the sourced ones. The register says which is which.

always match the code.

## Quick start

```bash
uv sync
uv run compute-options download          # all sources + FRED rates -> data/raw/*.parquet
uv run compute-options aggregate         # composite series per GPU -> data/processed/
uv run compute-options showcase          # structural curve, credit, quotes, governance -> docs/ + README
uv run compute-options price configs/products/capital_protected.yaml
uv run compute-options backtest configs/products/buffered.yaml --series composite_index --instrument B200
uv run pytest                            # unit tests; `-m network` hits live endpoints
```

Three sources only publish today's snapshot and the Ornn free tier serves a rolling three
months, so **run `download` daily** (cron or a scheduled job) to build up history. Storage upserts,
so reruns are safe.

To price the notes on the structural curve instead of the flat forward, set
`forward_model: structural` and `structural_gpu: H100_SXM` under `market:` in a product config.

### 1. Forward curve with no forward market

No compute forward trades, and a GPU-hour can't be stored, so spot and forward are not linked by arbitrage. The curve is built from the economics of supply: the price reverts to the cost of running a GPU (plus a margin), that cost level drifts down as newer generations arrive, and the rate at which a scarcity premium decays is learned from the index. Every input is a range in an [assumptions register](#assumptions-register), the ensemble of curves is reweighted by the observed index (Bayesian update), and the pricing stack uses the posterior mean.

### 2. Credit-linked transactions

Counterparty default is modelled with hazard-rate curves bootstrapped from CDS spreads. Exposure comes from Monte Carlo paths of the compute price, and default is linked to the price through a Gaussian copula, so the same code prices **wrong-way risk** (default likelier when we are most exposed). Three trades use it:

- **Prepaid capacity**: pay upfront for GPU-hours; the supplier's default strands the undelivered hours.
- **Take-or-pay offtake**: a counterparty commits to buy a volume at a fixed price; we are exposed when the market is below their price.
- **GPU-backed loan**: collateral value moves with the compute price, so default and recovery are linked.

| counterparty | 1y CDS (bp) | 3y CDS (bp) | 5y CDS (bp) | 5y default prob. | hazard 0-1y | hazard 3-5y |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hyperscaler (IG) | 30 | 50 | 65 | 5.4% | 0.005 | 0.015 |
| Neocloud (HY) | 600 | 780 | 855 | 52.0% | 0.099 | 0.170 |
| AI lab (unrated) | 1100 | 1400 | 1500 | 72.6% | 0.179 | 0.296 |

Only the neocloud 5-year level (about 855 bp, August 2026) is from a public source; the other two names and all curve shapes are illustrative. Recovery is a standard 40%.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

The secured loan is cheap against unsecured CDS at low loan-to-value, then reprices sharply as the collateral cushion thins, and the correlation matters more the higher the LTV.

### 3. Reservation bid and offer

A bilateral request has no market price to refer to. The quote is built from the risks being carried and each piece is reported: structural mid, a charge for model uncertainty (width of the posterior), a capital charge on the 99% VaR of holding the position unhedged (no listed contract to hedge with), credit (CVA with wrong-way risk), and a minimum edge. Charges are policy parameters (`quoting.engine.HURDLE`, `MODEL_RISK_MULT`, `MIN_EDGE`), not estimates.

![Ladder](docs/figures/bid_offer_ladder.png)

| counterparty | bid | mid | offer | width % of mid |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| AI lab (unrated) | $3.125 | $3.496 | $3.686 | 16.1% |
| Neocloud (HY) | $3.205 | $3.496 | $3.686 | 13.8% |
| Hyperscaler (IG) | $3.306 | $3.496 | $3.686 | 10.9% |

A quote memo, generated from the same numbers:

> **H100_SXM, 1,000,000 GPU-hrs/month, months 1-12, counterparty: Neocloud (HY).** Two-way $3.205 / $3.686 per GPU-hr around a structural mid of $3.496. The posterior 5-95% range for the mid is $3.115-$3.889, so we charge $0.118 for model uncertainty. There is no listed contract to hedge with, so we carry the position unhedged and charge $0.037 (offer) / $0.032 (bid) for the capital it ties up. Credit costs $0.000 if we sell to them and $0.106 if we buy from them. Total width $0.481 (13.8% of mid).

### 4. Structured notes on the same stack

The six structured notes price off the same `MarketState`, so they pick up the structural curve, the posterior volatility and the issuer's hazard curve with no change to the products. Solved terms on H100_SXM (2026-09-26):

| note | solved term | flat | schwartz | structural | forward/spot at maturity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| buffered | participation | 0.198 | 0.733 | 0.522 | 0.97 |
| capital_protected | participation | 0.364 | 4.127 | 1.161 | 0.94 |
| dual_directional | barrier | 0.974 | 0.972 | 0.919 | 0.97 |
| risk_reversal | call_strike | 1.088 | 1.004 | 1.034 | 0.97 |
| tracker | participation | 1.008 | 1.019 | 1.039 | 0.97 |
| zero_coupon | redemption | 1.056 | 1.056 | 1.056 | 0.87 |

Backwardation lowers the forward the options are priced on, which moves every solved term (a call struck at spot is out of the money against a lower forward, so it takes more participation to fund the same budget). Issuer credit matters too. Capital-protected 6M note, funding spread taken from each issuer's hazard curve:

| issuer | funding spread (bp) | participation |
|---|---|---|
| Risk-free | 0 | 84.4% |
| Hyperscaler (IG) | 30 | 95.6% |
| Neocloud (HY) | 593 | 308.0% |
| AI lab (unrated) | 1076 | 495.1% |

![Participation](docs/figures/note_participation.png)

Across posterior draws the solved participation runs from 26% to 977% (5th to 95th percentile) around 122% on the posterior-mean curve, and in 8% of draws no participation reaches the issue price (the draw's forward is too backwardated to fund the protection). That spread is parameter risk the issuer carries and a quote would price in.

The biggest movers are judgment inputs with low confidence (rent decay, margin, utilisation), and not the sourced ones. Rank correlation across the prior says the same (top five: margin +0.73, kappa -0.34, capex +0.34, util -0.26, life -0.17).

**Walk-forward test.** At each past origin the model is refit on the data available at that date and its forecast is compared with the index 30 and 60 days later.

![Walk-forward](docs/figures/walk_forward.png)

| GPU | horizon | origins | bias structural | bias flat | RMSE structural | RMSE flat | 90% band coverage |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| H100_SXM | 30 days | 11 | +0.030 | +0.022 | 0.054 | 0.050 | 82% |
| H100_SXM | 60 days | 8 | +0.065 | +0.051 | 0.078 | 0.067 | 88% |
| B200 | 30 days | 11 | +0.028 | +0.010 | 0.041 | 0.034 | 100% |
| B200 | 60 days | 8 | +0.056 | +0.024 | 0.068 | 0.052 | 100% |

**Interpretation.** Over this sample the index rose, the structural curve forecasts decay, and so it is biased low and loses to 'no change' in 4 of 4 GPU-horizon cases. The 90% band covers 82%-100% of outcomes (a few origins, overlapping windows, so the coverage figures are rough). Seven months of one regime can't validate a curve meant to describe 1-3 years. The test points to the cap on the long-run margin as the assumption doing the damage (see the revision log). Until a listed contract produces settlement prices, the model-uncertainty charge in the quote covers this risk.

**Revision log**

- **v1 -> v2**: margin high 0.60 -> 1.20, central 0.25 -> 0.30; kappa low 0.5 -> 0.1. Walk-forward test: v1 forecasts were biased low by 4-10% at 30-60 days because the market paid 1.9x (H100) to 2.5x (B200) levelised cost, above the cost-plus-60% cap, and the data showed no fast reversion. Widening cut H100 60-day RMSE from 0.100 to 0.078. Caveat: the same 7 months of data motivated and scored the change, so it is in-sample.

```

├── data/                 1. INGESTION

│   ├── aggregate.py      provider de-duplication, composite level, price index (time-product dummy)
```

## Modelling notes

- **Price on futures, not spot.** GPU-hours can't be stored, so there's no cash-and-carry link
  between spot and forward. Black-76 prices off the forward curve. A tracker's participation at
  par is `1 / (DF · F/S)`, so contango costs the investor. The difference between the forward and
  spot plays the role that dividends and carry play for equities.
- **Posted prices vs transactions.** Posted list prices are sticky. Most providers go months
  without changing, so posted-price composites have low volatility (about 8 to 16% on H100) and
  move in steps. Ornn's transaction index has about 74% daily volatility, much of it noise that
  reverts within days (about 39% on weekly returns). Price options on the benchmark that futures
  will settle on (Ornn/ICE, Silicon Data/CME, Compute Desk/Architect) and use composites for
  longer history and cross-checks.
- **Price index.** The level composite jumps when a cheap provider starts or stops being quoted.
  `composite_index` fits `log price = date effect + provider effect` over all prices at once (the
  time-product-dummy method), with residuals clipped so a single bad print has limited effect.
  Day-to-day chaining and median-based indices were tried and rejected: posted prices are
  sticky, so medians ignore the few providers that reprice, and chaining misses moves made while
  a provider is absent. Both showed H100 flat or falling from March to September 2026, while
  the providers quoted on both dates rose a median 15%. The index shows +17%.
- **Volatility.** Use `vol_horizon_days: 7` to estimate from overlapping weekly returns. The
  two-factor model (persistent level plus transient noise) separates the two on long samples, but
  a few months of data barely identify the long-run volatility, so `--sigma-long` lets you set it
  for synthetic runs.
- **Replication is tested.** Each product states its term-sheet payoff and its replication by
  legs. Tests check that the two agree, that Monte Carlo matches the closed form, and that the
  solver reaches par.
- **Issuer hedge.** The issuer delta-hedges with a futures contract expiring at the note's
  maturity, marked at the model forward. Hedged P&L = day-one margin + hedge error (gamma, vega
  from volatility re-estimation, gap risk). There is no basis risk yet, because no real futures
  settlement prices are available.

## Products

All amounts are fractions of notional. `R` = index at maturity / index at issue.

| Product | Payoff | Replication | Default solved term |
|---|---|---|---|
| Zero Coupon Note | `redemption` | zero-coupon bond | redemption (the issuer's yield) |
| Capital Protected | `protection + p·(min(R,cap) − K)⁺` | zero-coupon bond + call spread | participation |
| Buffered | `1 + p·(min(R,cap) − 1)⁺ − g·(1 − b − R)⁺` | zero-coupon bond + call spread − put | participation |
| Dual Directional | up: `1 + p·(R−1)`; `B ≤ R < 1`: `2 − R`; `R < B`: `R` | zero-coupon bond + calls + put(1) − 2·put(B) − 2(1−B)·digital put(B) | participation / barrier |
| Risk Reversal | `1 + p·(R − Kc)⁺ − (Kp − R)⁺` (funded or unfunded) | (zero-coupon bond) + call − put | call strike |
| Tracker | `p·R` | prepaid forward | participation |

## Limits and next steps

What this does not do:

- **No listed-futures or option prices exist in the data.** Volatility comes from the index, the
  forward is a model, and no basis between a hedge instrument and the book can be measured.
  Fitting to quotes needs `GridVolSurface` plus `black76.implied_vol` once any exist.
- **The cost model rests on sourced but secondary figures.** Several inputs are the analyst's
  range with no public evidence (utilisation, margin, rent decay, end-of-life value). The register
  flags each one, and the tornado shows how much each matters.
- **Only the neocloud 5-year CDS level is sourced.** The IG and unrated archetypes and the shape of
  every curve are illustrative. Own credit (DVA) is ignored, collateral and netting beyond a single
  netting set are not modelled, and the loan's default is reduced-form, not driven by covenants.
- **The walk-forward test is short and in-sample for the v2 priors.** Treat it as a
  diagnostic, not a validation.
- **Policy parameters** (hurdle rate, model-risk multiple, minimum edge) are inputs, not
  estimates.

Next:

- Adapters for paid and exchange sources (Ornn API key, Silicon Data, ICE/CME/Architect settlements)
  and a re-run of the walk-forward test as history accumulates.
- Physical-delivery structures: swing and take-or-pay with volume flexibility, calendar strips and
  averaging forwards, locational and quality basis.
- Jump-diffusion for step-like posted prices; smile once option quotes exist.
- Portfolio view: VaR and expected shortfall on a book of forwards, notes and credit-linked deals,
  with hedge-ratio studies against proxy instruments.
- Daily-monitored barriers and autocallables on the notes.
